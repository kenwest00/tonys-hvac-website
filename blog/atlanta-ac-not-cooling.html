Atlanta AC Not Cooling? Do These 6 Checks Before You Call Anyone

A practical Atlanta homeowner checklist for an AC that is running but not keeping the house cool, plus clear signs it is time to call a professional.

An AC that is running but not cooling your home can feel like an emergency in an Atlanta summer. Before you assume the system needs to be replaced, there are a few safe, practical checks that can help you understand what is happening.

Start with the thermostat

Confirm that the thermostat is set to cool, the fan is set to auto, and the selected temperature is below the current room temperature. A programming change, a mode change, or a setting that looks right at a glance can create a lot of confusion.

Check the air filter and airflow

An overdue filter can restrict airflow and make a system work harder. Look for vents blocked by furniture, closed supply vents, or a return-air grille covered by boxes, curtains, or dust buildup. These simple conditions can contribute to rooms that feel warmer than the thermostat setting.

Look at the outdoor unit from a distance

Make sure the outdoor unit has open space around it. Grass clippings, leaves, stored items, and dense vegetation can reduce airflow around the equipment. Do not remove panels or attempt electrical work. The goal is simply to notice obvious blockage.

Pay attention to what the system is doing

Is the equipment starting and stopping repeatedly? Is one room much warmer than the rest of the home? Is the air coming from vents weak? These details help identify whether the next step should be a performance review, a repair discussion, or a more urgent service call.

Know when to stop troubleshooting

If you notice a burning smell, repeated breaker trips, ice on the equipment, new mechanical sounds, or complete loss of cooling, stop guessing and call a professional. Those situations are not good candidates for a do-it-yourself fix.

Get a clear picture before the next heat wave

If the system is operating but underperforming, a professional maintenance review can be a practical next step. Coil condition, refrigerant performance, capacitor condition, blower-motor amperage, airflow, and visible wear can all affect comfort and reliability.
